How GrowthLens estimates school performance, gaps, and uncertainty

A short technical note for district leaders, data analysts, and curious skeptics. Covers what GrowthLens computes, where the numbers come from, and the assumptions that limit how strongly you should read them.

1 · What is being estimated

The residual

For each student in a given subject, GrowthLens ingests a standardized growth residual that is pre-computed upstream by the Missouri DESE / MOSIS growth model — the difference between the student's observed score and the score the state model predicts from prior achievement and grade, already expressed in standard-deviation units (the state's Z_RESIDUAL). GrowthLens does not fit this residual model; it aggregates and shrinks the residuals the state supplies. A school × grade × subgroup cell aggregates these standardized residuals.

The gap

A gap in GrowthLens is the within-school difference in mean residual between two subgroups at the same school, signed focal − reference — for example FRL minus non-FRL, so a negative gap means the focal group (FRL students) has the lower mean residual. It is not a difference in raw achievement, and it is not a difference relative to the state — both subgroups in a gap are measured against the same district baseline. This is why the district-wide gap can be small even when raw achievement disparities are large.

The district-wide number

The district-wide gap is a random-effects pooled mean over the per-school estimates, weighted by the inverse of each school's combined sampling and between-school variance:

w_i = 1 / (SE_i² + τ²) μ̂ = Σ w_i · r_i / Σ w_i SE(μ̂) = 1 / √(Σ w_i)

where r_i is the school's raw gap, SE_i is its within-school standard error, and τ² is the estimated between-school variance component (see §3). The 95% confidence interval shown on the overview card is μ̂ ± 1.96 · SE(μ̂).

2 · Shrinkage (empirical Bayes)

School-level estimates are shrunken toward the district-wide gap. The shrunken estimate at school i is a precision-weighted average of the school's raw estimate and the district mean:

B_i = τ² / (τ² + SE_i²) ← shrinkage factor r̂_i = B_i · r_i_raw + (1 − B_i) · μ̂

The shrinkage factor B_i ranges from 0 to 1:

  • B_i = 1 — the school's raw data is fully trusted; the estimate is unchanged from the raw value. Achieved only in the limit of zero within-school sampling error.
  • B_i = 0 — the school's data is entirely overridden by the district mean. Approached when the within-school SE is much larger than the between-school SD.

Most schools in a typical district sit between 0.6 and 0.9. The B column on the forest plot reports this directly.

The shrunken estimate's standard error is SE(r̂_i) = √(B_i) · SE_i — the posterior SD treating the district mean μ̂ as known. The full empirical-Bayes SD that also accounts for the uncertainty in μ̂ is about 1–2 % larger at typical district sizes; GrowthLens reports the μ-known form, the standard empirical-Bayes-caterpillar convention.

Important and counter-intuitive. Shrinkage pulls every school toward the district mean — including schools whose raw gap is smaller in magnitude than the district average. A school with a raw gap of 0.00 SD and a district-wide gap of −0.20 SD will end up with a shrunken estimate that is slightly negative. This is mathematically correct under the model — it reflects that with limited data, our best guess about the school is somewhere between its own (noisy) estimate and the district norm — but it can read as the model "inventing" a gap. Use the Raw / Shrunken toggle in Gap Analysis to compare the two side by side, and rely on the credible interval rather than the point estimate when a school is small.

3 · Variance components and τ²

The within-school cell standard error

The state supplies a per-student measurement SE s_i for each residual, which most pipelines lack. GrowthLens uses it as a precision floor without double-counting. For a cell of n students with sample variance S² and mean measurement variance ms² = (1/n) Σ s_i², the cell standard error is

SE_cell = √( max(S², ms²) / n )

When there is real between-student heterogeneity (S² ≥ ms², the common case) this reduces to the classic √(S²/n); when a small or homogeneous cell's sample variance understates the true noise, the state's measurement variance acts as a floor, so we never claim more precision than the measurements support — and we never double-count measurement error, since it is already embedded in S². A gap combines its two cells in quadrature: SE_gap = √(SE_A² + SE_B²).

Between-school variance τ²

The between-school variance τ² is estimated by REML (restricted maximum likelihood) fit of the random-effects model

r_i = μ + u_i + e_i u_i ~ N(0, τ²) ← between-school e_i ~ N(0, SE_i²) ← within-school sampling

The between-school SD reported in the Overview card is τ = √τ². It is the spread of true per-school gaps if the model is correct — not the spread of the observed point estimates, which is larger (because of sampling error) for raw estimates and smaller (because of shrinkage) for shrunken estimates.

If τ² is small relative to the average within-school SE², schools are mostly indistinguishable from one another: most of the apparent spread is sampling noise. If τ² is large, schools really do differ.

τ² is found by a bounded one-dimensional maximization of the REML profile log-likelihood over τ² ≥ 0 (a golden-section search, anchored by the closed-form DerSimonian–Laird estimate). Schools whose cells fall below the minimum-n threshold (§4) are excluded from the τ² fit and the pooled mean — their gaps are too noisy to inform the variance component — but they still appear in the figures, shrunk toward μ̂. Excluding the high-SE tail can bias τ² slightly low; this is a known, documented limitation.

4 · Minimum-n threshold

Every cell (school × grade × subgroup) is checked against a minimum sample size, n_min, which defaults to 10 in this build (a configurable threshold is planned for a future release). Cells with fewer students are flagged as below threshold. The threshold is intended to track your district's student-privacy and small-cell-suppression policies.

Threshold modeWhat it does
sectionBelow-threshold schools are moved to a labeled group at the bottom of the forest plot. Default.
inlineBelow-threshold rows stay in their sorted position but are dimmed and tagged with an n* badge.
hideBelow-threshold rows are removed from the figure entirely. The district-wide statistics still include them.

A higher threshold (e.g., n ≥ 30, used by many state report cards for public-facing tables) is appropriate when GrowthLens output will leave the analyst's workstation. The default of 10 is suitable for internal triage but is not a suppression rule.

5 · The two figures

Gap Analysis (forest plot)

One row per school for a single subject and subgroup pair. Point estimate, 95% credible interval, and shrinkage factor are all shown. The district-wide gap is overlaid as a vertical reference line. Use this when you want to see whether a gap concentrates in a few schools or replicates system-wide.

System Scan (heatmap)

A school × grade matrix of standardized residuals. Cells are colored on a diverging scale around zero. Use this before drilling into a specific gap — to find schools and grades that are consistently above or below expectation, and to see whether the system shows a coherent pattern (e.g., 4th-grade weakness across the district).

6 · What GrowthLens is not

  • Not a value-added model. The state's residuals condition on prior score and grade, but no teacher-, classroom-, or program-level effect is estimated. Results should not be used to evaluate individual teachers.
  • Not causal. Estimates describe where gaps and residuals are observed. They do not identify what is causing them.
  • Not a state report card. Cell suppression rules differ across states. Cross-check before any public release.
  • Not robust to extreme model misspecification. If the district's true growth pattern is wildly nonlinear or interacts strongly with covariates we don't include (e.g., mid-year mobility), residuals can be misleading. GrowthLens is a triage tool, not an end-of-line judgment.

7 · Privacy and data handling

All computation runs in the user's browser via DuckDB-WASM, which is vendored and served from the same origin as the app — no third-party or CDN request is made when you load a file, and the analysis works fully offline. The source CSV is parsed and queried entirely in the tab; it is never uploaded to a server, and no identifier, aggregate, or partial result is transmitted off the device. You can verify this in your browser's developer tools: the Network panel shows no off-origin traffic during analysis. Reload the tab and the data is gone — GrowthLens keeps nothing between sessions and uses no cookies or local storage for student records.
